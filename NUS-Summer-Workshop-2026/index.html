The project

Real-Time Video Analysis and Rendering

Group 5's entry into the Visual Computing project competition. We built a single webcam-driven system that analyses a live video stream and renders responses in real time — then gave it a purpose: accessible communication.

Everything we learned across three difficulty levels converged into a single product concept: SignVerse — a real-time interface that lets Deaf and hard-of-hearing users communicate with an AI through sign language, and lets AI respond visually.

SignVerse lets Deaf and hard-of-hearing users talk to AI in sign language — creating a more natural, independent and accessible digital experience.

Level 1 · Beginning

Face detection: from hand-crafted features to deep learning

The system's front end had to find and track a face reliably, in real time, on a laptop webcam. We started with the classic Haar Cascade + LBF landmark approach and compared it against the modern, lightweight YuNet detector (with MediaPipe landmarks).

Feature Classical Haar Cascade Deep learning YuNet
MethodHand-crafted featuresDeep-learning features
Head rotationLess reliableMore robust
Lighting changesSensitiveMore stable
Partial occlusionOften failsBetter performance
Real-time useFastLightweight and fast

Takeaway — the deep-learning detector kept tracking through rotated heads, uneven lighting and occlusions, at a speed that still allowed real-time use. That robustness is what made everything downstream possible.

Level 2 · Expert

Facial-expression recognition with a hybrid model

The core research question: can we read emotion better by combining what a face looks like with how the face is shaped? We built a hybrid model that fuses local CNN appearance features with geometric landmark features, and evaluated it against each part alone.

1

Detect & crop

SCRFD detector locates the face; a 192×192 crop is extracted and normalised (grayscale + normalisation).

2

Landmark extraction

Facial landmarks are located on the original image — eyes, brows, nose and mouth.

3

Local features (CNN)

Regions cropped by keypoints are passed through a CNN to give an appearance feature vector μ.

4

Geometric features

Keypoint geometry is normalised (shape-norm and z-score) into a second feature vector ν.

5

Fuse & classify

μ and ν are concatenated into a mixed feature vector π and classified by an MLP into 7 classes.

Classes: Angry, Disgust, Fear, Happy, Sad, Surprise, Neutral.

Level 3 · Bonus

Dancer recognition, dance scoring & gesture control

The bonus level pushed the same real-time pipeline into two harder problems: who is dancing, and how well are they dancing — then into a game you control with your hands.

Dancer recognition

YOLOv8s-pose provides per-frame bounding boxes, keypoints and a track ID. A three-step tracker then decides, over time, who is genuinely dancing.

1

Update track history

Record each track's last-seen frame and register new tracks.

2

Score calculation

Template score (frame similarity), activity score (body speed) and reliability score (track age + joint visibility).

3

Promote / evict

Add a dancer when the scores clear the thresholds; evict when motion drops out.

reliability = 0.55 × track age (capped 1.5 s) + 0.45 × recent joint visibility (last 1.0 s)
total = 0.15 × template + 0.55 × activity + 0.30 × reliability Evict a dancer after 1.2 s out of frame or total < 0.30 for 1 s; promote a new dancer when template > 0.45 & activity > 0.15 & total > 0.40.

Dance scoring engine

A teacher video is sampled at 15 FPS into a pose sequence, then aligned against the live performer with an optimal action-alignment search.

Total Score = 0.60 × Pose + 0.25 × Motion + 0.15 × Timing Pose = angles 60% + coordinates 40% · Motion = direction 70% + magnitude 30% · Timing: lag ≤ 0.15 s scores full, fading to ~0 by 0.8 s. Scores are smoothed exponentially.

Hand-gesture game control

Finally we turned the pipeline into play. MediaPipe hand landmarks are tracked frame by frame; the detected open/closed hand state drives rotation control, and an Angry Birds-style slingshot responds to gestures instead of a mouse.

This level was about latency: the feedback loop only feels natural when detection, decision and rendering all stay inside a single frame budget.

The bigger picture

SignVerse — A World Beyond Words

Technology is only worth what it unlocks for people. The final message of our project was simple: the same real-time video analysis that powers a demo can remove a real communication barrier.

SignVerse allows Deaf and hard-of-hearing users to communicate with AI through sign language, creating a more natural, independent and accessible digital experience.

By reducing communication barriers, SignVerse promotes digital inclusion and helps ensure that advances in AI are accessible to everyone — regardless of hearing ability.

HELLO I want COFFEE I am tired I NEED HELP I am SICK

Vocabulary recognised in the demo — short, practical phrases signed into the camera and matched against a reference gesture set.

Step 1–2

Capture & extract

An input gesture sequence is processed by MediaPipe Hands into 2D/3D hand keypoints per frame.

Step 3

Represent as a sequence

Frames become a T × (21 landmarks × 3 coordinates) feature sequence over time.

Step 4–7

Match & understand

Dynamic Time Warping compares the input against a gesture database, finds the optimal alignment path, and outputs the meaning — e.g. “HELLO” or “I NEED HELP”.

Life at the workshop

Three and a half weeks in Singapore: lecture halls and lab rooms, long build sessions, and a final demo day in a signature NUS orange polo shirt.

Reflection

What I took away

Beyond the certificate, the workshop changed how I think about building computer-vision systems under real constraints.

01

Real-time is a design constraint, not a detail

Every modelling choice had to survive a webcam and a frame budget. Latency shaped architecture more than accuracy did.

02

Classical vs deep, decided by evidence

Benchmarking Haar against YuNet taught me to let the failure cases — rotation, lighting, occlusion — pick the method.

03

Fusion beats any single feature

Combining appearance and geometric features, and reading them through heatmaps and t-SNE, showed me how hybrid models earn their keep.

04

Build for someone

The most motivating part was SignVerse: turning a technical demo into something that could widen access for Deaf and hard-of-hearing users.

Three and a half weeks, four teammates, three universities, one camera — and a much better sense of how to turn research models into systems that actually run.
